# Steel Joist Load Testing

## Quick Reference

|Exclusion zone|Loading: stay 6 in. from the specimen. Crane operation: stay 2 ft. from the load.|
|-|-|
|PPE, always|Safety glasses for everyone in the high bay during a test. Add gloves and safety shoes for rigging/setup work.|
|Crane operator|Shop personnel only, no exceptions.|
|Loading rate|\~0.1 in/min unless the work plan specifies otherwise.|

## What This Covers

Procedure for loading an open-web steel joist to failure (or a target load) on the strong floor: simply supported on a pedestal and roller, laterally braced, loaded through a point load on the top chord, with deflection and load recorded on LVDTs and a load cell.

Not covered: building custom fixtures, testing anything other than steel joists, or project-specific details (specimen count, sizes, target loads). Those live in the project's work plan, which points back to this SOP for the how.

## Before You Run a Test

* Complete the high bay lab safety Canvas module.
* Enerpac pump/hydraulics: get trained before you touch it.
* Untrained personnel may watch from a safe distance, not the work area.

## Who Does What

* Test supervisor: plans the test, sets load levels, can stop the test at any point, approves any deviation from this SOP.
* Shop personnel: run the crane, assist with rigging and fixture setup.
* Researchers/students: build the setup, wire instrumentation, run the DAQ, and execute loading under the supervisor's direction.

## Materials and Equipment

* Steel joist specimen and specimen identification/test plan
* Lateral braces, threaded rods, bolts, nuts, washers, and required hand tools
* Joist supports and support-adjustment hardware
* Loading beam and roller-type load-transfer supports, when required
* Load cell(s) and associated instrumentation, already installed
* LVDT(s), LVDT support/arm, cables, and data-acquisition computer
* Approved crane and rigging equipment
* Approved hydraulic or other loading system specified for the test
* Camera and measuring tools for post-test documentation, when needed

## Potential Hazards and Required Controls

|Hazard / Task|Harm and Controls|
|-|-|
|Suspended joist/loading beam during crane movement|Struck-by/crush hazard. Authorized operator only; maintain exclusion zone; never stand under a suspended load.|
|Fitting the joist between lateral supports|Pinch/crush hazard. Keep hands clear, make small controlled adjustments, communicate before moving.|
|Adjusting supports while the joist is held|Unexpected movement or loss of support. One person stabilizes, one adjusts; communicate clearly.|
|Tightening/loosening brace bolts|Injury if the wrench slips or the fastener releases suddenly. Correct tool/direction, stable footing, controlled force.|
|LVDT, load-cell, and data cables|Trip hazard, cable damage. Route and secure cables away from walkways and exits.|
|Testing, failure, and unloading|Sudden movement or stored energy. Maintain exclusion zone; don't approach until unloaded and declared safe.|
|Overhead stored specimens near load cells|Head-strike hazard. Use designated routes; wear head protection where required.|
|Rusted or rough steel surfaces|Cuts and abrasions. Wear gloves; avoid sharp or rough edges.|
|Clutter, removed covers, tools, hardware|Trip/fall hazard, blocked egress. Keep walkways and exits clear.|

## Procedure

### Setup

1. Review the test plan: specimen, support locations, load points, instrumentation, loading protocol, loading beam.
2. Bring braces, rods, supports, and tools from storage; keep unused items clear of walkways.
3. Open access locations for threaded rods; store removed covers clear of walkways and egress routes.
4. Position lateral braces loosely for final alignment; don't fully tighten until the joist and loading system are positioned.
5. Place joist supports and the LVDT support/arm per the test plan.
6. Set up LVDT instrumentation and the DAQ computer clear of pinch/overhead hazards; route cables to avoid tripping and interference.
7. Coordinate with the crane operator to position the joist; keep nonessential personnel outside the exclusion zone during movement.
8. If a loading beam is required, place it using the same crane and exclusion-zone controls.
9. With the specimen stable, align joist, supports, and loading beam to the test geometry; verify load-cell/loading-beam centerlines and roller-support bearing points.
10. Complete final brace alignment and tighten hardware; braces should restrain lateral movement without unintended clamping or preload.
11. Position the LVDT on the intended contact point (typically the top chord), verify it's vertical with adequate travel.
12. Check the DAQ display: load and displacement channels active, stable, and physically reasonable. Fix any zeroing issue before loading.
13. Complete the pre-test safety check and a verbal test-team check before beginning.

### Testing

1. Verify the specimen/test ID in the DAQ and start recording before applying any load.
2. Clear the exclusion zone; confirm no one is touching or positioned near components that could move.
3. Authorized operator applies load per protocol and rate; test supervisor monitors specimen, instrumentation, and data, and stays in communication with the operator.
4. Stop loading immediately on any unsafe condition, unexpected movement, instrumentation problem, or abnormal noise/motion. Resume only after the issue is evaluated and the area is declared safe.
5. Continue only to the defined test endpoint or stop criterion; personnel stay outside the exclusion zone throughout.

### Unloading and Post-Test Removal

1. After the endpoint, the operator reduces load in a controlled manner per lab procedure; avoid abrupt unloading unless required for emergency response.
2. Keep personnel outside the exclusion zone until load is removed, movement has stopped, and the test supervisor declares it safe.
3. Remove the LVDT only after the specimen is unloaded and stable; release spring-loaded mechanisms carefully.
4. Loosen brace hardware with controlled force and safe body position; keep hands clear of closing gaps.
5. Coordinate with the crane operator to remove the joist; reinstate the exclusion zone during removal.
6. Once the specimen is stable and safe to approach, take required photos/measurements and document the failure mode.
7. Return equipment to storage, reinstall access covers, remove trip hazards, and leave the area clean.
8. Confirm test data are saved per the project's file naming/storage procedure.

## Don't

* Push past the target/expected failure load without supervisor sign-off.
* Stand in the exclusion zone once the specimen is loaded.
* Tighten braces to the floor before they're in final position.
* Run the crane or pump without training. Call Larry for the crane, every time.
* Crowd, touch, or photograph the joist during or right after unloading.
* Loosen a stuck bolt with steadily increasing force. Expect it to release suddenly.

## Stop-Work Conditions

Stop the test if any of the following occurs:

* Unexpected movement of the specimen, supports, braces, loading beam, load cell, or rigging.
* Supports, braces, or fasteners become loose, damaged, or improperly seated.
* Instrumentation readings appear inconsistent with the physical response.
* Communication between the test supervisor and equipment operator is lost.
* The expected failure load is reached and further loading has not been approved by the supervisor.
* Any unexpected condition develops that may affect test safety.
